This reference gathers the Key terms tables of Chapters 1–14 and of Argument Chapters A and B, followed by the procedures of Chapters 3 and 4 and the notation that several chapters share. Each table appears under its chapter’s heading and lists the concepts that chapter explains principally: each term with its common alternatives, and its meaning or the distinction that matters most. The reference is meant to be consulted while reading rather than read through: a term’s first use, its worked example, and the reasoning around it stay in the chapter that teaches it. Chapters 1–9 are available on the website; the tables for Chapters 10–14 point forward to chapter readings that are not yet available.

A few conventions govern the rows. Names that pick out the same concept share one entry, so mean and arithmetic mean appear together, and outcome, response, and dependent variable are explained as one analytical role. A more specific role, such as treatment, exposure, or confounder, carries additional design or substantive meaning that the study itself must supply. Related concepts with different meanings, such as mean and median or SD and SE, keep separate entries. A term is listed under the chapter that explains it principally rather than the chapter that first mentions it, so a brief early introduction and a later full account can coexist: Chapter 1 introduces cases and samples through its studies, while Chapter 2 develops their use in reading records and calculations. Argument Chapters A and B, How to Read a Quantitative Study as an Argument and Extending the Argument-Based Approach to Quantitative Studies, extend the reading of one question-level argument to several statistical strands and coordinated questions; the notation table abbreviates them as Arg. A and Arg. B.

Procedures and Their Conditions

The table below gathers, for each procedure that Chapters 3 and 4 teach, what its result estimates and the conditions it relies on. Secured by design lists the conditions a study’s design can supply, such as random assignment, random sampling, or pairing; Model assumptions and how to check them lists the conditions the statistical model adds and the evidence available about each; If a condition fails gives the response the chapters teach; and Chapter names the chapter that develops the procedure. The choices made for one particular analysis, such as the sidedness of a test, the confidence level, or the family of comparisons, stay with that analysis in its chapter.
